# Memphis Artisan Keycap Set

## Creative premise

This is a coordinated set of four sculptural artisan keycaps for the keyboard's isolated 2×2 navigation cluster. Together they form a tiny Memphis-inspired installation: bold primary and secondary colors, playful geometric relief, deliberate asymmetry, and a slightly toy-like personality refined by clean construction and a glossy finish.

The set should feel like four related objects rather than four copies. Each keycap has its own composition, but they share the same visual vocabulary: rounded solids, architectural steps and arches, black graphic accents, and a restrained palette drawn from the keyboard's green, red, blue, yellow, and lavender details.

The original navigation legends are intentionally absent. These are decorative objects first, occupying a visually separate part of the board where their identity can come from form rather than text.

## The four keycaps

### 1. Arch and bead — upper left

An ivory or warm-cream base with scattered black terrazzo-like speckles. A thick teal semicircular arch rises from the surface, framing a small glossy red-orange sphere near its center. The composition suggests a doorway, rainbow, or abstract face without resolving into any one symbol. It is the lightest cap in the set and acts as a visual bridge back to the keyboard's ivory alphanumeric keys.

### 2. Zigzag and violet bead — upper right

A saturated mustard-yellow base supports a chunky black stepped zigzag, like a tiny lightning bolt or bent architectural beam. A lavender-violet sphere sits near the upper corner, deliberately offset from the zigzag. The tension between the dark angular shape and soft round bead gives this cap its character.

### 3. Disc and rails — lower left

A deep cobalt-blue base carries a raised warm-cream disc toward one side, balanced by three short parallel black rails. It can read as an abstract control panel, musical notation, or a sun beside horizontal shadows. This is the most graphic and restrained member of the group.

### 4. Lavender staircase — lower right

A vivid red-orange base holds a lavender staircase or stepped ziggurat rising diagonally across the surface. The steps are broad, rounded slightly at their edges, and feel architectural rather than typographic. This cap has the strongest sense of upward movement and completes the set with a clear sculptural silhouette.

## Shared form language

- Use visibly raised, tactile relief rather than printed graphics.
- Favor softened edges and friendly radii; nothing should feel sharp or aggressive.
- Let the geometry remain crisp and intentional, with enough negative space for each composition to read at keycap scale.
- Keep the four bases different in color but consistent in profile, gloss, and overall visual weight.
- Treat black as a precise graphic accent, not a dominant base color.
- Repeat lavender sparingly so the artisans relate to the keyboard chassis.
- The set should feel playful, collectible, and designed—not chaotic or novelty-driven.

## Material impression

The render suggests opaque cast resin or carefully finished multi-part resin pieces. A polished satin-to-gloss surface would suit the colorful toy-object quality, while slight translucency could be reserved for the small spherical elements. The terrazzo speckles on the cream cap may be embedded flecks or painted inclusions. Exact fabrication methods and engineering constraints can be decided during technical development.

## Relationship to the keyboard

The artisans concentrate complexity in the isolated upper-right cluster while leaving the main typing field calm. Their palette echoes the colored modifier keys without matching them mechanically: cobalt connects to Alt, red-orange to Control, teal/green to Shift, yellow to the rhombus keys, and lavender to the chassis. This makes the board feel composed as a whole while allowing the artisan block to be its expressive focal point.
